# Aurora Flux

Build me a cel-shaded arcade aurora racing game in Three.js that takes place across an infinite night-sky cosmos filled with flowing aurora rivers, celestial currents, drifting nebulae, meteor storms, shattered moons, and colossal star-born creatures. The game should achieve the level of visual quality, mechanical polish, and artistic cohesion expected from a modern AAA studio release. I do not want a demo, prototype, proof of concept, or technology showcase. I want a finished, beautiful, highly polished game that people would willingly pay for and play repeatedly.

Use Bun, Three.js, TypeScript, and ES modules. The entire project must run immediately with `bun install` followed by `bun run dev`. There should be no additional setup, configuration, asset downloads, or manual steps. The experience should launch directly into a playable race.

Use absolutely zero external assets. No downloaded models, textures, HDRIs, sounds, fonts, particle atlases, or visual resources of any kind. Every mesh must be generated procedurally in code. Every texture must be generated procedurally through shaders, noise functions, or canvas rendering. Every sound effect and audio system must be synthesized through the Web Audio API. If a resource is not generated by the codebase itself, it does not belong in the project.

Target modern desktop and laptop browsers, including integrated-GPU and GPU-constrained devices. The game should scale gracefully from high-end hardware to modest systems without sacrificing visual identity. Maintain a stable frame rate through adaptive quality systems, dynamic resolution scaling, aggressive batching, instancing, frustum culling, object pooling, shader complexity scaling, configurable particle budgets, and a real LOD system. Performance is a primary feature rather than a post-launch optimization task. The game must automatically adjust rendering quality based on measured performance and remain enjoyable even on hardware with limited graphics capability.

The entire visual style must be anime-inspired non-photorealistic rendering. Take inspiration from Guilty Gear Xrd, Promare, Redline, and Studio Trigger's use of color, energy, and motion. Avoid physically based rendering entirely. Nothing in the final image should read as realistic. Every material, lighting choice, effect, and color decision should feel illustrated, graphic, intentional, and stylized.

Implement a custom cel-shading pipeline with hard lighting bands using ShaderMaterial or an equally flexible approach. Use three or four discrete lighting bands with nearest-neighbor filtering and carefully tuned thresholds. The lighting must be artistically tuned rather than relying on default values. Every racer, obstacle, structure, creature, and environmental object must use inverted-hull outlines with screen-space-consistent thickness. Outlines should remain visually stable regardless of viewing distance. Add a screen-space edge-detection pass using a normal and depth prepass combined with a Sobel filter so that interior linework appears naturally throughout the scene. Use both systems together and tune them carefully to avoid double edges.

Apply strong Fresnel rim lighting to racers, celestial creatures, obstacles, and major environmental structures so silhouettes remain readable against the luminous environment. Use hard-banded specular highlights rather than smooth reflections. Simulate environmental response through ramps, gradients, or stylized techniques rather than physically accurate reflections. Create a massive stylized sky system featuring infinite stars, drifting nebulae, distant planets, animated constellations, celestial fog layers, and a living aurora-lit atmosphere. Choose a limited palette built around emerald, cyan, violet, deep-space blue, and starlight white, then apply it consistently across the entire game.

The aurora system is the heart of the project and should receive more attention than any other subsystem. The race does not occur on roads, tracks, or traditional surfaces. Racers travel along enormous rivers of living light flowing through space itself. These aurora rivers should feel alive, dynamic, and majestic. Build them using layered procedural flow fields, animated turbulence, large-scale directional movement, and localized energy variations. The player should always understand the direction and motion of the current. The world must feel infinite. The horizon should never reveal boundaries, seams, loading zones, or visible repetition. The environment should continuously reposition around the player so that the illusion of endless space remains intact.

Aurora rendering should use hard-banded color transitions driven by energy density, current intensity, flow velocity, and elevation variation. Avoid smooth gradients whenever possible. Layer the aurora with animated energy streaks, drifting particles, wake effects, drift trails, collision shockwaves, speed-line effects, and glowing ribbon structures that reinforce the anime aesthetic. The aurora should feel closer to a living cosmic river than a visual effect.

Populate the cosmos with procedural large-scale landmarks and hazards. Create vast crystal forests composed of enormous floating crystalline structures that form high-speed corridors and technical racing sections. Generate shattered moon fields containing drifting debris and broken celestial fragments. Place massive luminous arches and cosmic structures throughout the world to create memorable silhouettes and navigation landmarks. Build drifting nebula banks that affect visibility and alter the visual character of sections of the course. Add enormous planets in the background that rotate slowly and provide a sense of scale.

Create gigantic celestial leviathans and star whales that migrate through the cosmos. These creatures are not enemies and should not behave like bosses. They are living parts of the environment. They cross race routes, move through aurora rivers, and create dynamic moments that make the universe feel alive.

The environment itself should serve as a major gameplay system. Generate meteor streams that periodically cross sections of the track and force players to react. Create lightning veins that travel through aurora currents and telegraph their paths before discharge. Add gravity wells that distort movement, influence steering, and create risk-reward shortcuts. Include dark matter storms that reduce visibility and affect control responsiveness. Introduce comet crossings that generate high-speed moving hazards. Add spatial fractures and tears in reality that distort visuals and alter navigation. These hazards should feel like natural celestial phenomena rather than traditional racing-game obstacles.

The game format consists of four racers, including the player and three AI opponents. Each race contains three laps, a countdown sequence, checkpoint validation, a finish line, split timing, position tracking, and a final results screen. Design an actual circuit rather than a simple loop. The course should contain high-speed straights, technical chicanes, narrow crystal corridors, gravity-well hairpins, meteor-crossing sectors, leviathan migration zones, and open acceleration sections that reward skillful navigation. Represent the racing line as a glowing energy ribbon generated from a CatmullRomCurve3 spline. The ribbon should feel integrated into the aurora system rather than floating above it. Add animated checkpoint gates, wrong-way detection, and corner-preview indicators.

Vehicle handling should be arcade-focused, fast, responsive, and satisfying. Take inspiration from F-Zero GX, Wipeout, and the best drift mechanics in arcade racers. Implement acceleration, braking, steering, boosting, drifting, air control, drafting, and collision recovery. Drifting should build energy that can later be converted into powerful speed boosts. Slipstreaming behind opponents should provide meaningful strategic advantages. Collisions should feel impactful without becoming frustrating. The racer should feel powerful and exhilarating to control.

The three AI racers should each possess distinct personalities. One should be aggressive and willing to take risks. One should be technical and optimized around clean racing lines. One should be erratic and unpredictable. Implement spline-following behavior with lookahead steering, drafting, overtaking, collision avoidance, shortcut usage, recovery logic, occasional mistakes, and mild rubber-banding. The player should feel challenged but never cheated.

Every racer must have a visible anime-style pilot. These characters should be procedurally modeled and animated entirely through code. They should lean into turns, shift their weight under acceleration, react to impacts, adopt different postures during boosts and drifts, display idle movement during calm sections, and perform celebrations after victory. A static character model will significantly reduce the quality of the experience, so character animation must be treated as a first-class system.

Create a cinematic chase camera with spring damping, dynamic look-ahead behavior, speed-based field-of-view adjustments, drift biasing, impact shake, and boost effects. Add cinematic orbit cameras during countdowns and results screens. The camera should communicate speed while remaining readable and comfortable.

Design a custom anime-inspired HUD rather than a generic HTML overlay. Display speed, lap count, race position, split times, boost energy, a minimap, hazard warnings, and race status information. Every interface element should match the game's cel-shaded visual language.

All audio must be synthesized through the Web Audio API. Generate engine tones, drift-charging sounds, boost releases, collision impacts, meteor flybys, lightning discharges, countdown effects, finish fanfares, and ambient cosmic soundscapes procedurally. No downloaded audio files may be used.

Structure development around specialized sub-agents. One agent should own the aurora rendering system. One should own the cel-shading and outline pipeline. One should own racer physics and handling. One should own AI behavior and race systems. One should own rider animation. One should own world generation and celestial hazards. One should own HUD, camera, and audio. One should own performance optimization. A dedicated visual critic should evaluate the game continuously and provide specific, actionable feedback rather than approval.

The performance agent should actively profile CPU time, GPU cost, draw calls, shader complexity, memory consumption, particle budgets, and post-processing overhead. It should continuously search for bottlenecks and provide fallback rendering paths for constrained devices. Every visual feature should have a graceful degradation path rather than simply being disabled.

Before building any gameplay systems, create a Playwright-based screenshot harness capable of loading the game, advancing it to specific states, and capturing screenshots from multiple camera angles and quality tiers. Every visual claim must be verified against captured frames rather than assumptions about how the code should look.

The visual critic should evaluate screenshots continuously and identify concrete defects such as repetitive aurora patterns, weak silhouettes, muddy cel-shading bands, lifeless character animation, empty horizons, poor obstacle readability, uninspired particle effects, or unconvincing environmental motion. Continue iterating until the critic can no longer identify meaningful defects.

Build the project in playable milestones. First create the aurora system and camera. Then implement racer movement and handling. Next complete the cel-shading pipeline. Then build the course and checkpoint systems. After that implement celestial hazards and dynamic environmental events. Then add AI racers. Then add riders and animation. Then complete the HUD and audio systems. Finish with a dedicated optimization pass and a final polish pass. Every milestone should remain fully playable.

Track progress honestly. If a subsystem is only partially complete, state that clearly. Do not describe something as finished simply because it functions. Functionality is the minimum requirement. The goal is a game that feels shipped.

The final deliverable should be a complete repository containing a README, clean architecture, readable code, documented shaders, a Playwright screenshot harness, procedural asset generation systems, and a fully playable game. When I run `bun install` and `bun run dev`, I should immediately enter a race across endless rivers of aurora beneath an infinite star-filled sky, weaving between meteor storms, crystal forests, gravity anomalies, spatial fractures, and migrating celestial leviathans. The game should automatically adapt itself to the available hardware while preserving its artistic vision. The result should look like a piece of published anime artwork brought to life and should feel polished enough that I want to race again immediately after finishing.
